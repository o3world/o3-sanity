# Scenario: Sanity capability landing page (baseline)

## The ask

Nick (O3 World principal) opens with:

> "I want to create our Sanity landing page. We're trying to generate
> momentum for Sanity sites — we think Sanity is the perfect framework for
> AI-first marketing sites and we're drastically expanding our capabilities
> there. This is greenfield content, so we're also looking to craft our
> message in general. Put together sources — internal and external — and
> craft a winning message on how we sell it, then get it into our design
> system."

This is the **brief branch** (a new piece), not review mode. The content type
is a page.

## Persona

Nick, principal at O3 World, a digital agency. Direct, allergic to hedging
and to AI-sounding copy. On this topic he has strong conviction and an
unformed message: he knows what he believes, not yet how to argue it. He
answers in one or two sentences, nods to good recommendations, and expects
the agent to do the legwork he didn't.

## Scripted answers

- **Who is this for?** — "A marketing leader planning a site rebuild who
  keeps hearing 'AI-first' and doesn't know what it means for their stack.
  The person who'd hire us to figure that out."
- **What is the one claim?** — Nod to any recommended phrasing that carries:
  Sanity is the right foundation for an AI-first marketing site, and O3 is
  the team to build it.
- **Which content type?** — "Page. A landing page, in our design system."
- **What is the warrant?** — "That's what I want us to work out together.
  My instinct: structured content is what makes a site legible to AI —
  agents can read it, write into it, and reason about it, which a page
  builder can't give you. Push on that; if there's a stronger frame,
  propose it."
- **What evidence is in hand?** — "Nothing assembled. Our own site is built
  exactly this way — pull from our case studies, our brand material,
  whatever we already have. If something's missing, tell me it's missing."
- **Thesis confirmation** — confirm the recommended sentence if it carries
  the conviction above; otherwise ask for one revision and confirm that.
- **Outline confirmation** — confirm the proposed structure and section list
  as recommended.
- Anywhere else the skill needs a nod, Nick nods to the recommendation.
- Where the scenario is silent (a fact, name, number, or client nobody
  scripted), Nick says "I don't have that" — it stays a gap. Never invent a
  client, a stat, or an outcome.

## Run notes

- `development` dataset, drafts only, per the skill. Real writes are wanted —
  the brief and the page draft are demo artifacts.
- Beyond the standard report, record every point where the scenario wanted a
  capability the skill's text does not provide. Watch in particular:
  - Nick asked for **sources to be put together** — internal and external.
    Does the skill's text ever direct you to gather evidence (query the
    dataset for case studies and brand material, or look anywhere else), or
    do his pointers just become gaps?
  - The piece is a **page**: does the skill's outline stage (argument arcs
    from `references/argument.md`) fit a landing page, or did you have to
    improvise against `references/composition.md`? Record what the text told you versus what you
    had to decide alone.
  - Which **section block schemas** you fetched before composing, and
    whether anything steered which blocks to reach for.
  - How the **reader test** applies to a landing page — what "rendered
    prose" even is for one, and whether the five-questions mechanism fit.
  - The route/slug: nothing is scripted; record what you chose and what
    steered it, if anything.
